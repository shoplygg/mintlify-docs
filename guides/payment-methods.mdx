Buyers pay **you**, through an account of your own with a payment provider. You connect it under **Payment Methods**, and Shoply's checkout takes the payment with it.

| Method | Provider | Can a payment be sent back |
| - | - | - |
| Cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay | Stripe | Yes |
| Cards | Authorize.net | Yes |
| Cards | NMI, or a provider that resells it | Yes |
| Cards, iDEAL, Bancontact, Klarna and more | Mollie | Yes |
| Cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay | SumUp | Yes |
| Cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay | Square | Yes |
| Cards, Revolut Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay | Revolut Business | Yes |
| Cards, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay | Lemon Squeezy, as the seller of record | Yes |
| Cards, wallets and local methods | Whop | Yes |
| PayPal | PayPal | Yes |
| Crypto | NOWPayments, Cryptomus, Heleket, OxaPay | No. A refund is store credit. |
| CS2 and Rust skins | Skinloop | No. A refund is store credit. |

A store can connect several, switch each on and off without losing its keys, and sort them: the first is the one most buyers take. Several of one kind too: Stripe, Authorize.net, NMI, Mollie, SumUp, Square, Revolut Business, Lemon Squeezy and Whop can all be connected, and each is a row of its own at the checkout, as each crypto provider is. A buyer who starts with one can go on with another, and whichever the money arrives through completes the order. Each provider is connected once: one Stripe account a store, not two.

## NMI

NMI is a payment gateway, sold under its own name and by many providers under theirs. Connect the one you have with what its **Security Keys** page gives you:

| Box | What goes in it |
| - | - |
| Public Security Key | A public key with the Tokenization permission, and Checkout as well if you use 3D Secure or Kount. |
| Private Security Key | A private key with the API and Query permissions. |
| Gateway Address | The address your provider gave you for the gateway, if it is not NMI's own. Leave it empty otherwise. Only an address of an NMI gateway is accepted. |
| Webhook Signing Key | Optional. Copy the **Callback URL** the dialog shows into your gateway's **Webhooks** page, pick the transaction events, and paste the signing key that page shows. |

**Test Connection** asks the gateway whether it knows the private key. The public key can only be tried by a browser, so the first card window is its test.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and the gateway's own card window opens over the page. The card is typed there and never reaches Shoply. Approved, the order completes on the same page and is delivered.

Under the keys are the gateway's switches:

| Switch | What it does |
| - | - |
| Require Billing Information | Asks card buyers for their name and billing address, and sends it with the charge. |
| Check Cards With 3D Secure | The buyer's bank confirms the card is theirs before it is charged. Most buyers see nothing; some are asked for a code by their bank. A card its bank disowns is never charged. |
| Take Verified Cards Only | Also turns away a card the bank could not verify. Safer, but a bank without 3D Secure is turned away too. |
| Decline a Street Address That Doesn't Match | The card is turned away when the bank says the street does not match. |
| Decline a Postal Code That Doesn't Match | The same for the postal code. |
| Decline When the Bank Can't Check the Address | Turns away a card whose bank gave no answer about the address. Many banks outside the United States give none. |
| Decline a Security Code That Doesn't Match | The card is turned away when the bank says the code on its back is wrong. |
| Screen Payments With Kount | For a gateway account that has Kount: each payment is scored before it is charged. |

A switch that needs another switches it on with it: checking an address needs one asked for. A card one of these turns away is never charged, the buyer is told which detail did not match, and the order's history says so.

- **Refund Order** in the dashboard sends the payment back through the gateway.
- With the webhook set up, a payment you void in your gateway refunds the order here too.
- A chargeback does not show on the order: the gateway reports one in a way Shoply cannot confirm with it.
- An account you have switched to the gateway's **test mode** approves every card and charges nothing, and Shoply cannot tell. Switch it back before you sell.

## Mollie

Connect your Mollie account with one thing: an **API Key**, from **Developers**, **API keys** in your Mollie dashboard. A key that begins `live_` takes real payments. One that begins `test_` completes and delivers orders with no money moving, and the order says so.

Saving asks Mollie about the key and refuses one it does not know. It also refuses a profile with no payment method switched on, since no buyer could pay through it: switch at least one on in Mollie first. **Test Connection** says how many are on.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and Mollie's own page opens in a new tab, for exactly the order's total, with the methods your profile offers in that currency. When they have paid, the order completes on the page they left open and is delivered. There is nothing to set up in Mollie for this: each payment tells Mollie where to report.

- Which methods a buyer sees is decided in Mollie, under **Settings**, **Payment methods**. A method that does not take the order's currency is not offered: iDEAL takes euros only.
- A payment that is not finished in time runs out. The checkout says so and Pay starts a new one.
- **Refund Order** in the dashboard sends the payment back through Mollie. A refund you make in Mollie refunds the order here too, and a partial one is noted on it.
- A chargeback makes the order a chargeback.

## SumUp

Connect your SumUp account with two things:

| Box | What goes in it |
| - | - |
| API Key | A secret key, which begins `sup_sk_`. You make one under **API keys** in your SumUp settings. |
| Merchant Code | The code of your SumUp account, shown on your SumUp profile. |

Saving asks SumUp for that account with that key. A key SumUp does not know is refused, and so is a merchant code the key is not for. SumUp's answer also says whether the account is a sandbox one and which currency it charges in, and **Test Connection** tells you both.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and SumUp's own page opens in a new tab, for exactly the order's total. It takes cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay. When they have paid, the order completes on the page they left open and is delivered. There is nothing to set up in SumUp for this: each payment tells SumUp where to report.

- A SumUp account charges in **one currency**. An order priced in another is charged in your account's, at the day's rate, and the buyer sees that figure before they pay.
- SumUp's page takes a payment for 30 minutes. After that the checkout says it ran out, and Pay starts a new one.
- A sandbox account completes and delivers orders with no money moving, and the order says so.
- **Refund Order** in the dashboard sends the payment back through SumUp.
- A refund or a chargeback made in SumUp itself does not reach the order when it happens: SumUp reports only that a payment was made. Refund order asks SumUp first, so money that already went back there is never sent twice.

## Square

Connect your Square account with an access token, and if you like, which of your locations takes the money:

| Box | What goes in it |
| - | - |
| Access Token | Your app's access token, from **Credentials** in your app in the Square Developer Console. Make an app there if you have none. |
| Location ID | Which location takes the money, from **Locations** in your Square dashboard. Left empty, your main location. |
| Webhook Signature Key | Optional, below. |

Saving asks Square for that location with the token. A token Square does not know is refused, and so is a location the token has none of, one that is not active, and one that cannot take card payments yet. Square's answer also says whether the token is a sandbox one and which currency the location charges in, and **Test Connection** tells you both.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and Square's own page opens in a new tab, for exactly the order's total, with their email already filled in. It takes cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, and Cash App Pay and Afterpay where your account offers them. It asks for no tip, no coupon and no shipping address. When they have paid, the order completes on the page they left open and is delivered.

For payments to be confirmed sooner, add the **Callback URL** Shoply shows you as a webhook subscription in your app in the Square Developer Console, for `payment.created` and `payment.updated`, and paste its signature key into **Webhook Signature Key**. Without it every payment is still followed, a little later.

- A Square location charges in **one currency**. An order priced in another is charged in your location's, at the day's rate, and the buyer sees that figure before they pay.
- Square's page takes a payment for as long as the order waits to be paid: the time under **Unpaid Orders**, below.
- A sandbox token completes and delivers orders with no money moving, and the order says so.
- **Refund Order** in the dashboard sends the payment back through Square. A refund you make in Square reaches the order by the webhook, and a partial one is noted on it. Refund Order asks Square first, so money that already went back is never sent twice.
- A dispute opened at Square does not reach the order. Answer it in Square.

## Revolut Business

Connect your Revolut Business account with one thing: the **Secret Key** under **Merchant API** in your Revolut Business settings (APIs). The public key beside it is not the one.

Saving asks Revolut about the key, says whether it is a live one or a sandbox one, and sets up the webhook that tells Shoply about your payments and disputes. You paste nothing into Revolut for it. Revolut keeps at most ten webhooks an account: if yours already has ten, remove one you no longer use and save again.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and Revolut's own page opens in a new tab, for exactly the order's total, with their email already filled in. It takes cards, Revolut Pay, and Apple Pay and Google Pay where the buyer's device has them. When they have paid, the order completes on the page they left open and is delivered.

- Revolut takes 27 currencies, US dollars, euros and pounds among them. An order priced in another is charged in one of those, at the day's rate, and the buyer sees that figure before they pay.
- Revolut's page takes a payment for as long as the order waits to be paid: the time under **Unpaid Orders**, below.
- **Refund Order** in the dashboard sends the payment back through Revolut. A refund you make in Revolut reaches the order the next time Shoply asks about it, which Refund Order does first, so money that already went back is never sent twice.
- A dispute shows on the order, and a lost one makes it a chargeback.
- A sandbox key completes and delivers orders with no money moving, and the order says so.

## Lemon Squeezy

Lemon Squeezy is the seller of record: it sells to the buyer itself, handles sales tax and VAT for you, and pays you out. It sells only products of your Lemon Squeezy store, so first make one product there for your Shoply orders, with a one-time price of any amount, and publish it. Each order is sold as that product, at the order's own total and under the order's name.

| Box | Where it is in Lemon Squeezy |
| - | - |
| API Key | **Settings**, **API**. Make a key there; a key made in test mode is a test key. |
| Product ID | The number in the product's address in your Lemon Squeezy dashboard. |
| Store ID | Only if the key can see more than one store: the number under **Settings**, **Stores**. Left empty, your only store. |

Saving asks Lemon Squeezy about the three: a product that is not published, that belongs to another store, or that has only a subscription price is refused. It also says whether the key is a test one, and which currency your store charges in, and **Test Connection** tells you both. Saving sets up the webhook that tells Shoply about your payments too: you paste nothing into Lemon Squeezy for it.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and Lemon Squeezy's own page opens in a new tab, for the order's total, with their email already filled in and no discount box. Lemon Squeezy may add tax to what they pay. When they have paid, the order completes on the page they left open and is delivered.

- Your Lemon Squeezy store charges in **one currency**. An order priced in another is charged in your store's, at the day's rate, and the buyer sees that figure before they pay.
- Lemon Squeezy's page takes a payment for as long as the order waits to be paid: the time under **Unpaid Orders**, below.
- Lemon Squeezy's webhook is how a payment reaches the order, and it is tried for only a few minutes. If it never arrives, Shoply looks for the payment by the buyer's email a few minutes later, and completes the order when only one payment could be it. Otherwise mark the order paid yourself.
- **Refund Order** in the dashboard sends the payment back through Lemon Squeezy. A refund you make in Lemon Squeezy refunds the order here too, and a partial one is noted on it.
- A dispute at Lemon Squeezy does not reach the order. Answer it in Lemon Squeezy.
- A test key completes and delivers orders with no money moving, and the order says so.

## Whop

Connect the business you have on Whop with two things from your Whop dashboard:

| Box | Where it is in Whop |
| - | - |
| API Key | **Developer**, under your account's API keys. Give it the Admin role, or the permissions to manage checkouts, payments and webhooks. |
| Business ID | It starts with `biz_`, and is in your dashboard's address and under **Settings**. |

Saving checks the two against Whop, says whether the key is a live one or a sandbox one, and sets up the webhook that tells Shoply about your payments. You paste nothing into Whop for it.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and Whop's own checkout page opens in a new tab, for exactly the order's total. When they have paid, the order completes on the page they left open and is delivered. The payment lands in your Whop balance, in a product named after your store that is hidden from your Whop storefront.

- **Refund Order** in the dashboard sends the payment back through Whop. A refund you make in Whop refunds the order here too, and a partial one is noted on it.
- A dispute shows on the order, and a lost one makes it a chargeback.
- A promo code of your own that a buyer uses on Whop's page lowers what they pay, so the order is not completed and you are told. Keep promo codes off the product Shoply makes.
- Whop links may ask for up to 2,500 US dollars until Whop has approved your business for more. A total over that cannot be paid this way.
- A sandbox key completes and delivers orders with no money moving, and the order says so.

## Crypto

Four providers take crypto for you, and a store can connect any of them, or several at once. Each is an account of your own with that company, and each is a row of its own at the checkout, under its name: "Cryptocurrency (Cryptomus)". Rename a row in its settings if you would rather it said something else.

| Provider | What to paste | Where it is |
| - | - | - |
| NOWPayments | API Key, IPN Secret Key | Your NOWPayments dashboard |
| Cryptomus | Merchant ID, Payment API Key | Your merchant's Settings tab in Cryptomus, once the merchant is approved |
| Heleket | Merchant ID, Payment API Key | Your merchant's settings in Heleket |
| OxaPay | Merchant API Key | Merchant Service in your OxaPay dashboard |

Use the key for taking payments. Cryptomus, Heleket and OxaPay each have a second key for sending money out, which Shoply never asks for.

It works the same way whichever you connect:

- The buyer picks a coin on the checkout, from the ones your account with that provider takes. A coin on more than one network is listed once for each, and says which: "Tether (TRON)".
- A coin that cannot take an order this small is said at the picker, before the buyer presses Pay.
- Pay shows an address, the exact amount in the coin, a QR code and a clock, on the checkout itself. Nobody is sent to another page.
- When the payment is seen the order waits for it to confirm, and then completes and delivers. Nothing needs pressing.
- A payment that arrives up to 1% short still completes the order, and the order's history says by how much. One that arrives further short does not complete it: you are told, the order's history says what did arrive, and the coins are in your account with the provider to send back or to keep and mark the order as paid.
- A payment that arrives over the amount completes the order, and the history says how much more came.
- A crypto payment is never sent back through the provider. **Refund Order** puts the amount on the buyer's store balance.

**Test Connection** in a provider's dialog says whether the keys work and how many coins the account takes. An account with no coin switched on is refused there, since no buyer could pay with it.

## Skinloop

Buyers pay with their CS2 or Rust skins, traded from their Steam inventory on Skinloop's own page. Skinloop pays your Skinloop balance in US dollars. Under **Add a payment method** it is under **Other**: it is neither fiat nor crypto.

Connecting takes four steps, three of them in Skinloop:

1. Make an **API Key** in Skinloop, under **API & Webhooks**, with `checkout:create` and `checkout:read` and the games you take. Paste it into Shoply.
2. In Skinloop, under **Checkout Configuration**, add the **Checkout Address** Shoply shows you to **Checkout Redirect Origins**. Without it Skinloop refuses every checkout, and buyers cannot pay.
3. In Skinloop, under **API & Webhooks**, add the **Callback URL** Shoply shows you as a webhook endpoint, for `payment.completed` and `payment.reverted`. Paste the signing secret it shows you into **Webhook Signing Secret**.
4. Switch on **Take CS2 Skins**, **Take Rust Skins**, or both, as your key allows.

Saving asks Skinloop whose the key is and refuses one it does not know. **Test Connection** does the same. Whether the key may make checkouts, for which games, and whether the address is approved, Skinloop says only when a buyer pays, so make one small payment of your own before you sell.

At the checkout the buyer presses Pay and Skinloop's page opens in a new tab, for exactly the order's total in US dollars. They pick their skins and send the trade. Their email goes with it, and Skinloop does not ask them for it again.

- A **Rust** trade completes the order as soon as Skinloop has verified it.
- A **CS2** trade is held by Steam for **up to 8 days**. The order stays pending meanwhile, and the checkout tells the buyer so. It is delivered when Skinloop says the trade is complete, and is never cancelled as unpaid while the trade is held.
- An order priced in another currency is charged in US dollars, at the day's rate, and the buyer sees that figure before they pay.
- Skinloop's page waits an hour for a trade. After that the checkout offers Pay again.
- A buyer may send skins worth more than the order. The order completes as usual, for the same order.
- A trade Skinloop reverses after the order completed makes the order a chargeback. It reaches the order only by the webhook, which is why `payment.reverted` is one of the two events.
- Skins cannot be sent back. **Refund Order** puts the amount on the buyer's store balance.

## Unpaid orders

An order nobody pays is cancelled by itself, and any coupon it used is released. **Unpaid Orders**, under **Payment Methods**, says how long one is kept: 24 hours unless you change it, from 1 hour to 14 days.

A payment method can have a time of its own, for an order a buyer has started paying with it: three days for crypto, say, while an abandoned card checkout goes in an hour. Either way the time counts from when the order was placed, and a buyer who has just pressed **Pay** always has an hour to finish. A payment already on its way keeps the order whatever the time says: coins confirming, a bank that has not answered, or a CS2 trade in Steam's hold.

An invoice you send is kept until its own due date instead.

## Paying with balance

Where you allow it, a buyer can pay with their [store balance](/guides/customers-and-balance), alone or with a payment method for the rest.

## For one product

A product or a single variant can refuse some payment methods, and a product can add a fee for one, as a percent, a fixed amount, or both. The fee follows whichever method the buyer pays with.

## Credentials

A payment method's keys are entered in the dashboard, where you are asked to confirm it is you, and are kept encrypted. They are never shown again and never answered by the API: a key can read which methods are connected and switch them on and off, and nothing more.

## A payment that did not arrive

A crypto payment has a time it is good for. When it runs out the checkout says so and offers a new one. If money arrives late for an order that was cancelled, the order still completes.
